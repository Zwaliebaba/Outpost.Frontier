PREVIEW · {{ stateLabel }}

The refinery is the first machine in this game that runs while you are away. A job is (recipe, batch): inputs leave the Bay the moment you commit, the shard cooks on its own clock — offline, through restarts (G1) — and outputs plus a deterministic refund arrive at completion. The screen's job is to make that commitment arithmetic you can read before you make it, and to teach the one map that matters: what this station can cook, what it could cook if everyone builds it up, and what it will never cook.

One ruling shapes the layout: no High-Sec station can ever cook Nova-Steel (bandTierCaps — high 2, low 3, null 4). The locked list is the industrial map — ore runs into the dark and alloys run out of it — so locked rows are explained, never hidden: hiding them would hide the reason to fly anywhere.

§2 DESIGN CALLS THE ADR LEAVES TO THE PRINT
LOCKED ROWS ARE THE INDUSTRIAL MAP
Every recipe is always listed, and a locked row states which lock and what changes it — two different sentences, deliberately. A tier lock points at the project board beside it ("TIER 2 — THE PROJECT BELOW"): buildable, communal, this screen's own loop. A band cap points out of the station entirely ("NEVER IN HIGH-SEC — LOW / NULL ONLY"), in amber, because it is geography, not progress: the sentence that sends industry into the dark is the whole endgame economy, and this row is where a player reads it for the first time.
THE FORM IS ARITHMETIC, SHOWN BEFORE COMMIT
Four lines, always the same four: INPUTS NOW (with the Bay's after-state), OUTPUT AT COMPLETION, TIME (with the batch and tier discounts named, not folded silently), REFUND (a tier fact, floored, deterministic — shown even when it is zero, because "TIER 2 WOULD RETURN 5" is the upgrade pitch in one line). No probability appears anywhere on this tab; nothing here rolls.
SLOTS ARE YOURS, NOT THE STATION'S
Slots and the queue of ten are per player (a shared pool is a griefable queue — the ADR put contention at the field and the trade lane instead), so there is no station traffic to browse and no queue-jumping UI to design. Other commanders' jobs are invisible, the same privacy the roster and the Bay already keep. RefineryBusy is therefore always about your ten, and the screen counts them down in plain sight.
OFFLINE IS THE FEATURE — THE SCREEN SAYS SO
The refinery is the game's first "walk away" loop, and a player who does not know that will sit and watch a 22-minute bar. The offline line is permanent chrome, not help text; ETAs are wall-clock, not session-clock; and a job finishing while away lands in the event record for the away log to report — this tab never owes a notification, which keeps it honest at any staleness.
THE PROJECT BOARD IS COMMUNAL AND IRREVERSIBLE
Contribution is the phase's only sink, it benefits everyone, and it cannot be taken back — three reasons the verb sits behind its own confirm while QUEUE does not. The board shows per-material bars, the contributor ledger (D19's record, names client-side like wing names), and — when two commanders race the last units — the preview clamps at what remains, because the project completes exactly once and the screen should never offer units it will not take.
§3 DELIBERATELY ABSENT · STILL OPEN
ABSENT BECAUSE THE DESIGN SAYS SO
▸ No fuel line — fuelPerJob is authored zero until a market can sell Ionized Slurry; drawing the row now would show a cost that never charges.
▸ No shared queue view — slots are per player; a station-wide job board would leak activity the roster and Bay deliberately keep private.
▸ No reverse recipe — alloys never melt back to ore; the compression is one-way by design, so no "reprocess" verb exists to draw.
▸ No quality or blueprint knobs — refund and time are tier facts from hash-guarded content; there is nothing on a job to optimise but its batch size, and that is the point.
OPEN — FOR THE PRINT REVIEW
▸ Job cancel. The ADR is silent. Proposal: a queued job cancels whole (inputs return to the Bay); a running job cannot cancel — its inputs are spent. Needs a ruling before E4 writes the verb, or its absence.
▸ What a job is on the wire. Shown as one batch per job, the queue holding many. If E4 spells (recipe, batchCount) as N batches in one job, the composer gains a count and the queue rows collapse — same screen, one field.
▸ Contribution granularity. Shown as whole stacks with a clamp at what remains. A partial-amount picker is the alternative; proposal is no — it is the only quantity keypad either cargo print would have.
▸ Where the away log lives. Completions land in D19's record; proposal: the alerts family owns telling the player (a toast on login), and this tab only ever shows current state.
