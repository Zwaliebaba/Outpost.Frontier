PREVIEW · {{ stateLabel }}

This tab is where ore stops being a hold and becomes property. E3 built the whole loop under it — manifests survive every crossing, the Bay is a per-(you, station) ledger that outlives the grid, and two verbs move stacks between them. The screen's job is to make one move fast and honest: dock, see what came home, commit it. It is a selection surface like the hangar, not an inventory of slots — and though ore is all a hold can carry today, ore is only the first item family: the tab's whole vocabulary is item stacks, so 08c's taxonomy grows it by rows and chips, never by redesign.

One ruling shapes the layout: transfers are manual, both directions (ADR-024 §5c) — the transfer is the risk decision and the commitment of ore to industry. Nothing auto-drains on dock, so the primary verb must be one tap from arrival, and everything else on the tab exists to make that tap informed.

§2 DESIGN CALLS THE ADR LEAVES TO THE PRINT
THE VOCABULARY IS ITEM STACKS — ORE IS ONLY THE FIRST FAMILY
Every readout on the tab is an (item, units, litres) triple: the manifest columns, the Bay's rows grouped by family, the MOVE chips, the fill preview. 08c already defines the growth path (`ItemTypeId`, six container kinds), so when alloys land at E4 — and whatever families follow — they join as rows in their group and chips in the filter, never as a new panel, and no label on this screen says "ore" where "item" is meant. One consequence is recorded now rather than met by surprise: per-item manifest columns hold to roughly five item types; past that the columns collapse to stacked chips per hull, which is a layout change this print pre-authorises.
UNITS ARE THE NUMBER, LITRES ARE THE COST
Every stack reads twice: units first (what the ledger counts, what recipes eat), litres beneath (what a hold spends). The load bar is property, not a gauge — ADR-017's no-gauges rule was about damage, and E3 amended it the same way: cargo is the player's property, so drawing it breaks nothing that rule protects. Unit volumes differ per ore (300/200/250 L), which is exactly why one number alone would lie.
TRANSFERS MOVE SELECTIONS, NOT SLOTS
No drag-and-drop inventory grid. The gesture is the hangar's: tap hulls (additive, across groups), pick an ore filter, one verb. Stack-wise means per ore, whole stacks — the wire's `MoveOre` takes an ore and a count, and the only computed split is fill-to-capacity, done by the pre-check. A slot grid would promise placement the roster-order rule does not keep.
DIRECTION FOLLOWS THE SELECTION
Loaded hulls selected → TO BAY is primary; an empty hull selected → TO SHIPS is primary, previewed as a fill. Both verbs are always visible and the inactive one is disabled with its reason, never hidden — one form, two signs, because they are one `MoveOre` with the sign flipped, and the screen should say so rather than pretend they are different systems.
THE BAY DRAWS NO METER
A Bay has no capacity in the content and no epoch to go stale against — what a command put there stays until a command moves it. A meter would invent a scarcity nobody designed, the same argument that kept the dock-capacity meter off P1. The Bay is three counts and their litres, full stop; its one adornment is the alloy stub naming what joins it at E4.
EMPTY HOLDS COLLAPSE TO ONE LINE
Sixty ships dock; three hold ore. This tab is about property, so hulls with nothing are one summary row with a SHOW, not sixty chips — the same economy that let P1 fit the hangar without a scrollbar. Industrials with empty holds stay visible individually (a Hauler reading READY is information: it is the fill target), combat hulls do not.
PENDING IS A MARK, NOT A GUESS
The command acks immediately; the confirming numbers arrive on the next ~1 Hz BayStatus/roster. In the gap the moved stack shows a ◌ beside its old value rather than an optimistic new total — a screen that guesses a ledger invites the ledger to disagree with it, and this corpus refuses client-side truth about property everywhere else too.
§3 DELIBERATELY ABSENT · STILL OPEN
ABSENT BECAUSE THE DESIGN SAYS SO
▸ No other commander's Bay — privacy is in the ledger's key; another player's Bay is not even addressable, so there is no shared-storage panel to design.
▸ No transfers in space — dock is the boundary. Jettison, tractor and ship-to-ship are combat-era questions, and drawing hooks for them now would invent mechanics.
▸ No rows for items that do not exist — BayStatus is three ore counts on the wire today. E4 adds alloys, 08c's taxonomy adds families beyond; each joins its group when it is real, and the stub reserves the space so nothing reshuffles.
▸ No quantity keypad — stacks move whole per ore; the only split the screen computes is fill-to-capacity, by the same pure function the server validates with.
OPEN — FOR THE PRINT REVIEW
▸ Ore order on a fill. Shown as ore-index order (F-C → AST → NEB); value-density order would favour the rare ores. Needs a rule before E5 — it is player-visible arithmetic.
▸ A dock shortcut. §5c makes the transfer manual, but "TRANSFER ALL from the fleet that just docked" as a one-tap toast action may be worth its convenience. Proposal: yes, as an explicit tap — still manual.
▸ Where CargoStatus surfaces. In-space holds (HOLD FULL) belong to the tactical roster strip, never this tab. Proposal recorded here so E5 does not double-home it.
▸ Pending treatment. Shown as ◌ beside the old value (no optimistic totals). The alternative — apply optimistically, reconcile on BayStatus — reads faster and lies occasionally. Needs agreement before E5.
