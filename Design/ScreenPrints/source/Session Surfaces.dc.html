This hand-off dangled on both ends. 06 §7 sends the login, character-create, queue and update-required screens to "the 07 placeholder's screen inventory"; 07 §4's inventory and the rescoped T-CLI-09 never received them — so nobody owned the first thing a player ever sees. These are client surfaces only: 06 remains a placeholder, so nothing here invents a credential policy (F2), a token lifetime, or an MFA posture. What it does fix is what the player is shown, and what each screen must be told by the service.

Standing on two decided answers. F1 — standalone accounts, own email and credential store, no platform SSO — means the client owns a real credential form rather than deferring to a platform overlay. F3 — exactly one character per account — collapses what would be a character-select list into a single resume card, and makes creation a first-run event rather than a repeated one.

§3 WHAT THIS ASKS OF THE SERVICE, AND WHAT IS STILL OPEN
REQUIRED FROM 06 / 04, OR THE SCREEN CANNOT BE BUILT
▸ Name availability as a live check, not a submit-time rejection — the create screen shows ✓ before BEGIN.
▸ Queue depth and drain rate, not just position. An ETA without a rate is a number nobody trusts twice.
▸ A structured away-log from the AI commander — orders it executed and losses taken, not a text blob. Screen 06 renders rows.
▸ Both schema hashes in the UpdateRequired reject. 04 §3.1 currently returns the rejection without saying what the server expected.
▸ Server status before authentication, so the login footer can be honest to an unauthenticated client.
OPEN
▸ 06 is still a placeholder. These surfaces are designed against its decided answers (F1, F3) and its binding constraints only. F2 credential policy and F4 MFA will each add a field or a step here.
▸ Starter systems are unauthored. Screen 03 shows three because a single option is not a choice — but 08 §5 owns what they are, and whether the player picks at all.
▸ Is REVIEW LOSSES a screen or a filtered journal? It implies a killmail surface no document has scoped.
▸ Registration is out of scope here — it is the project's only unauthenticated write endpoint (06 §2.1) and its abuse posture is a security design, not a layout.
