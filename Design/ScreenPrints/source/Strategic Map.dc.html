Stacking them was the obvious move and it is wrong. Sovereignty is a categorical fill, activity is a continuous one, and superimposing them makes a contested high-traffic system indistinguishable from a quiet one under a different owner — the two facts a commander most needs to separate. One overlay at a time, switched by a key or a tap, keeps every colour on the screen answering exactly one question.

§3 THE ROUTE PLANNER'S PROBLEM R3's finding, in the one screen where it bites
A 14-JUMP ROUTE INTO A 4-SLOT QUEUE
03 §9.6 promises a route planner and 03 §9.3 promises waypoint chains with per-leg ETAs. But 02 §5.1's OrderQueue holds four slots. A fourteen-jump route has nowhere to live.
Three ways out, and they are not equivalent: widen the queue (touches the hot replication path and 02 §3.3's capacity maths); make a route a single order the server expands; or keep the route client-side and feed the queue one jump at a time.
Decided: the third — the map plans, the client feeds. No schema change, no server work. The consequence is accepted rather than hidden: a disconnected player stops at the next gate, so 02 §8's AI commander inherits a fleet mid-route with no further waypoints, and 07e's reconnect screen must say so — "held at TAL-4, 9 jumps remaining" is the difference between a bug and a rule.
WHAT THE PLANNER OFFERS
▸ Three routes, always — fastest, safest, and the one avoiding hostile sovereignty. Presenting one route hides the only interesting decision on the screen.
▸ Per-leg security, not a route average. A single 0.1 system in an otherwise safe chain is the whole risk, and an average erases it.
▸ Avoid-list is persistent and survives re-planning — a system you refuse to fly today you refuse next week.
▸ F15 makes this honest: with a complete universe copy the planner never shows a route through space the client has not seen, so there is no "unknown" state to design.

DEPENDS ON CONTENT THAT DOES NOT EXIST
▸ Constellation layout is a legibility problem, not a physics one — the universe proposal says content may lay systems out for legibility. This map assumes clustered constellations with clear gaps; if the bake produces even scatter, the hulls and labels here stop working.
▸ System naming is root-index, so labels are short and dense by construction — the map is designed against that, and a switch to long proper names would need a different label strategy.
▸ Two security values (region band, per-system number) both appear here and must stay distinguishable: the band is a badge, the number is per-system.
§5 THE SITE LAYER ADR-024's sites on the 4X surface · fifth overlay · added 2026-08-21

ADR-024 gave every system 2–3 mining sites and split their truth in two: archetype and grade are bake content — public and stable, showable to everyone exactly like security — while the remaining pool is presence-gated: you know how eaten a field is only because a ship of yours stood in it this epoch (§3d: "seeing without presence is intel's territory"). The layer draws that split instead of papering over it, and the daily epoch — pools refill, bearings rotate, layouts reshuffle — is the expiry rule for everything the player once knew.

Unknown is not full. A system with no pool data draws no depletion state and the panel says so — the map never guesses a ledger (the CARGO tab's ◌ rule, applied to geology). And when a site's epoch turns, yesterday's number is dropped rather than greyed: a re-formed field contains none of the ore the stale reading described, and a map that kept it would be drawing yesterday's rocks.

TWO TRUTHS, TWO TREATMENTS
Archetype and grade render for all 48 systems from the client's complete bake copy — F15 again: no unknown state to design. The pool renders only from your own SiteStatus receipts, per epoch, and is dropped — not greyed — at re-form. A dropped number beats a wrong one, and the split teaches the intel game: anyone can see where the grade-V pocket is; knowing how much is left costs a ship standing in it.
GEOMETRY NOW, HUE WITH D-C1
One phosphor family carries the layer: grade is brightness, archetype is diamond geometry — filled, centre-dot, dashed — appearing only at ≥20 px per 07b's legibility floor. Region zoom shows count and brightness alone, because a 6 px pip cannot honestly say which ore it is. When D-C1 authors the ore hues they join the pips without a redesign — and colour was never the only signal, so the colour-vision palettes inherit the layer for free.
THE MAP SELLS THE SYSTEM, NEVER THE SPOT
A site's bearing is the epoch's to derive and rotates daily (§3d) — drawing it here would promise a warp-in the next epoch moves. The layer marks presence-in-system only; the system view owns today's position. Countdowns read EPOCH ≥ rather than a schedule: an occupied world holds its epoch (R27), so the clock is a floor, not a promise.
FIFTH OVERLAY, STILL EXCLUSIVE
A grade-V triple under hostile sovereignty is the most interesting object on this map precisely because it is two facts. §2's rule holds: ore is a magnitude, ownership a category, and stacked they hide each other — the miner's question and the fleet commander's question get one keypress between them, not one screen. Ownership mutes to dim chrome under this layer so nothing borrows the standings hues.
NEEDS A DECISION
▸ Whose presence counts? v1 proposes your own ships only. Corp-shared scouting is intel design pulled forward — attribution, spoofing, staleness — and this screen already ruled intel pings carry reporter identity for exactly that reason.
▸ Faded High-Sec pockets (ruling 1b): tagged FADED in the panel as proposed, or their own pip state? The tag teaches the geography lesson only after a click.
▸ Where the scouting journal persists — device-local or account-side. The same question 07h left open for settings, now with gameplay stakes: a second tablet that forgot your scouting is a real loss.
GROUNDED — ZERO NEW WIRE
▸ SiteStatus already carries it — per-ore remaining, per-cluster fractions, the epoch, ~1 Hz to viewers of that grid. The journal is client-side memory of messages already sent.
▸ The epoch countdown is derived from (anchorId, epochIndex) — the same pure function both halves already evaluate. Zero bytes.
▸ Archetype, grade, orbit ring are baked — in the complete copy every client holds (F15). The layer's only new data is what the player's own ships saw.
