This screen answers "where in this system, and how long to get there." The strategic map answers it between systems; this is the level below, and it is the only surface where a player picks a destination by looking at a place rather than at a row in a list. Everything on it is an anchor, a fleet, or scenery — and the first two are the only things that can be clicked.

One rule shapes the layout: ships warp to anchors, never to coordinates (ADR-016 §3), so an anchor is drawn as a target and everything else is drawn as backdrop. That is why the rings are not to scale and never will be: this screen is a pick list arranged as a place, and the only distance it states is time.

The ETA is on the screen because the layout deliberately cannot say distance. Rings are ordered by the bake's anchor order and evenly spaced, so a player reading the picture learns adjacency and nothing else. The number that tells them what a trip costs comes from the same spool-and-transit arithmetic the tactical ghost prints, so the two surfaces cannot disagree about one warp — and there is no scale bar anywhere, because a scale bar over a non-linear layout is a lie with a ruler drawn on it.

§2 DESIGN CALLS THE ADR LEAVES TO THE PRINT
ANCHORS ARE TARGETS, EVERYTHING ELSE IS BACKDROP
ADR-016 §3 already decided that ships warp to anchors and never to coordinates, and this is that rule made visible: an anchor is drawn with a target reticle and a label, and a star, a moon or an unanchored body is drawn dimmer, unlabelled and inert. The player never learns the rule by clicking something and being refused — the screen says what is clickable by how it draws it, which is the same reason the command row greys a verb rather than hiding it.
SITES ARE A FOURTH ANCHOR KIND, AND THEY MOVE
ADR-016 predates mining. `AnchorKind::Site` landed with E1b, so the system view draws fields beside planets, stations and gates — and they are the one anchor whose position is a function of the day. The client needs no message for it: `SiteEpochPlacement` is pure, deterministic and client-callable, so the screen computes today's field from the bake and the epoch index. A view whose epoch has rolled draws the new place, never yesterday's rocks.
WARP IS THE TACTICAL GRAMMAR, UNCHANGED
Clicking an anchor composes the same `OrderKind::Warp` the tactical surface composes, through the same pre-check, the same pending ghost, the same ETA label and the same bounce with the same reason string. No second validation and no second vocabulary — a refusal here reads exactly as a refusal there, which is ADR-005 §4's parity claim applied to a surface rather than to a link. The screen contributes the destination and nothing else.
FLEET MARKERS ARE COUNTS AT PLACES
They come from the summary family at ~1 Hz — a count and a state per anchor — and never from a snapshot, because a system view that drew hulls would need the grid it is not standing on. An `InTransit` row slides along its route line by presentation-only interpolation between updates: honest about where it is going, approximate about where it is between two truths, and never past the arrival the summary has not yet reported.
VIEWING IS NOT BEING THERE
Focus never gates command (ADR-016 §7), so this screen opens for any system and orders warps for fleets in it whether or not the player is watching that grid. It is a "which system am I looking at" screen, not a "where am I" one — the header names the system, never the player. The only thing presence changes is what the tactical surface would show on the way through, and the remote badge says so rather than a second layout doing it silently.
ONE HOP FROM HERE, ROUTES FROM THE MAP
A warp issued here is a single hop to an anchor in this system, including its gates. Multi-hop planning — SET DESTINATION, ADD WAYPOINT, the route line across systems — stays the strategic map's, because that is where the route solver's answer is legible. The split keeps `UniverseRoute` with one caller and stops two surfaces owning one plan.
§3 DELIBERATELY ABSENT · STILL OPEN
ABSENT BECAUSE THE DESIGN SAYS SO
▸ No orbital motion — bodies do not drift. Orbital periods are content nobody authored, and a click target that moves under the cursor is a worse screen than a still one.
▸ No scale bar, no distances — the rings are order, not length; a ruler over a non-linear layout would be a lie with authority.
▸ No other commanders' fleets — presence gates what you learn about the live world, and a system-wide traffic list would leak exactly what the roster and the Bay keep.
▸ No combat, sovereignty or intel layer — none of that content exists, and drawing a legend for it would design it by accident.
OPEN — FOR THE PRINT REVIEW
▸ Ring spacing past eight anchors. Even spacing by bake order is legible to about eight; the committed universe has systems with more. Needs a rule — an outer belt, a second column, or a pinch.
▸ Where sites sit. Shown on their own outer ring, because a field is a place you go rather than a body you pass. The alternative is placing them by their real orbit ring among the planets.
▸ One marker or two at a mixed anchor. A station with three docked and six on grid is drawn here as one marker with a split count. Two markers reads more precisely and crowds the ring.
▸ Does a gate show the far side's name? Drawn here as `GATE → KIL-7`, which the bake knows. Naming it turns the system view into a one-hop map; hiding it makes the player open the map to answer "where does this go".
